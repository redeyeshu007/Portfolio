# Building MeVo AI: Real-Time Meeting Insights with AI

Most meetings end the same way: everyone agrees on next steps, and within a day half of those steps are forgotten. I built **MeVo AI** to fix that — an assistant that sits inside a Google Meet session and turns conversation into <mark>live summaries, action items, and insights</mark> as people talk.

The harder, and more meaningful, part was accessibility: MeVo also recognizes sign language from the webcam and converts it to text in real time, so deaf and hard-of-hearing participants can take part on equal footing.

## What it does

- **Live summaries** that update as the discussion moves, not after the call.
- **Action items** with owners pulled straight from the transcript.
- **Sign-language recognition** that lets gestures join the conversation as text.

## How it works

- Audio is streamed and transcribed continuously, then fed to an LLM with a rolling context window.
- A separate computer-vision pipeline (TensorFlow + OpenCV) handles the webcam feed for gesture recognition.
- Summaries and action items are regenerated incrementally so the UI never blocks.

## What I learned

Real-time AI is mostly an exercise in latency budgets. The model quality matters less than how quickly and smoothly you can show *something* useful. Streaming partial results beat waiting for a perfect answer every time.

Accessibility wasn't a feature bolted on at the end — designing for it first made the whole product clearer.
