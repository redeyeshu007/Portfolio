# My Journey Through Hackathons: From Early Failures to Top 1% Nationally

Multiple high-intensity competitions later, I've experienced both ends of the spectrum: watching demos crash minutes before judging in my early days, to securing **Top 1% nationally in the AWS ML Challenge**, **2nd Place at Robovanza**, **2nd Place at HackVerse 2.0**, and podium finishes across South India.

Both sides taught me things no lecture hall ever could.

## Milestones That Shaped Me

- **AWS ML Challenge (2026)**: Competed nationally against thousands of engineering teams, engineering deep learning pipelines to rank in the **Top 1%**.
- **Robovanza (2026)**: Designed and pitched an end-to-end hardware-software prototype under strict 24-hour constraints, taking **2nd Place**.
- **HackVerse 2.0 (2026)**: Built a functional rapid prototype to take **2nd Place**.
- **Sarvam 26 & Titanium 26 (2026)**: Secured **3rd Place** at EASA and **4th Place** at RIT Chennai competing with inter-college talent.

## What Winning Teams Do Differently

- **Scope ruthlessly**: The team that builds *one* complete, bulletproof flow beats the team that half-builds five disconnected features.
- **Demo early, demo often**: If your MVP isn't working by the halfway mark, your pitch will suffer.
- **Lead with the human problem**: Judges evaluate the value of the solution and the clarity of execution, not how many libraries you installed.

## The Real Takeaway

Hackathons taught me to make decisive engineering trade-offs, ship under uncertainty, and maintain calm when production breaks. Those skills carry directly into every client platform and software system I build today.
