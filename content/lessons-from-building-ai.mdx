# The Biggest Lessons from Building AI Applications

AI demos are easy. AI *products* are hard. Most of what I learned building real systems with TensorFlow, OpenCV, and LLMs came from the gap between those two things.

## Lessons that stuck

- **Data beats cleverness.** A simpler model with good data outperformed a fancy one with messy data, every time.
- **The model is 20% of the work.** Data pipelines, edge cases, and the UI around it are the other 80%.
- **Latency is a feature.** A correct answer that arrives too late is a wrong answer in a real-time app.
- **Garbage in, confidently wrong out.** Models fail *loudly and convincingly*, which is more dangerous than failing visibly.

## Practical takeaways

- Start with the **simplest model that could work**, then earn the right to add complexity.
- Build **evaluation in early** — you can't improve what you can't measure.
- Design for the **moment the model is wrong**, because it will be.

## Wrap-up

Working on accessibility tools, meeting AI, and computer-vision systems taught me that AI is engineering, not magic. The teams that win aren't the ones with the biggest models — they're the ones who handle the unglamorous 80% well.
